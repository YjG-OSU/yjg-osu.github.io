Yunjing Guo

Ph.D. Candidate in Operations Research

Department of Integrated Systems Engineering
The Ohio State University · Columbus, Ohio

I develop optimization models to study how electricity and water infrastructure can support a low-carbon energy system. My research focuses on coordinated investment and operation of power systems, desalination-based water systems, and energy storage.

I am advised by Ramteen Sioshansi and co-advised by Chen Chen. My work connects operations research, power systems, and energy economics, with applications to grid flexibility, ancillary services, and long-term infrastructure planning.

My background in electrical engineering includes research on hybrid AC/DC distribution networks and investment decisions for power systems with energy storage. This foundation informs my current work on the interactions between electricity and water infrastructure.

Expected Ph.D. completion: December 2026.

Research

My broader research interests include optimization, market and mechanism design, and decision-making for sustainable and reliable infrastructure. I am particularly interested in how operational flexibility and coordination across energy and water systems can inform investment decisions and energy policy.

Coordinated power and water planning

I develop mixed-integer nonlinear models to examine how coordination between electricity, desalination, and water distribution systems affects investment and operation under carbon-emissions limits. Convexification and reformulation help make these models computationally tractable.

Infrastructure planning · MINLP · Convexification

Water-system flexibility and energy storage

I compare the flexibility of water distribution systems with conventional energy storage over short- and long-term planning horizons. This work combines a mixed-integer quadratically constrained model with a scalable clustering approach for representative-day modeling.

Decarbonization · MIQCP · Long-term modeling

Water systems in ancillary-service markets

I study the value of reserve provision by desalination-based water systems. The modeling accounts for the feasibility of reserve activation while maintaining water-service requirements.

Electricity markets · Reserve provision · Water-service feasibility
