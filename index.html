Yunjing Guo

Ph.D. Candidate in Operations Research

Department of Integrated Systems Engineering
The Ohio State University · Columbus, Ohio

My research combines operations research, power systems, and energy economics to support deep decarbonization. I develop optimization models grounded in engineering principles and system data to study investment and operation across interconnected infrastructures. My doctoral work focuses on electricity and desalination-based water systems, examining how flexible water demand, water storage, and energy storage can support reliable, affordable low-carbon power systems. More broadly, I aim to uncover flexibility across sectors and translate it into practical planning, operating, and market decisions.

I am advised by Ramteen Sioshansi and co-advised by Chen Chen.

Outside of research, I enjoy hiking, photography, boxing, and theater.

Research

Current research

My dissertation uses electricity–water systems as a platform for studying infrastructure flexibility. Desalination and pumping consume electricity, while water tanks allow production and delivery to be shifted in time. My three projects examine how these capabilities affect planning, storage needs, and reserve provision.

Coordinated power and water planning

I compare separately operated and jointly coordinated systems using models that retain the physical operating constraints of electricity and water networks. My results highlight the growing value of coordination as carbon limits tighten: shifting water loads, using tanks, and aligning capacity expansion can become especially important in the later stages of decarbonization.

Infrastructure planning · MINLP · Convexification

Water-system flexibility and energy storage

I compare the storage-like capabilities of reservoirs, desalination, and pumping with batteries and pumped storage, examining when water infrastructure can complement or partially substitute for conventional energy storage. I also develop tractable planning methods that capture hourly variability, daily operations, and longer-term storage effects under different carbon targets and renewable-energy levels.

Decarbonization · MIQCP · Long-term modeling

Water systems in ancillary-service markets

I model reserve provision by desalination-based water systems while maintaining water-service requirements. The work accounts for hydraulic behavior, response requirements, storage interactions, and operating limits to assess whether promised reserves can actually be delivered. It connects physically feasible operation with participation in electricity markets.

Electricity markets · Reserve provision · Water-service feasibility

Future directions

Coordination across timescales and decision-makers

I plan to connect short-term market participation with long-term investment through models that better represent hydraulic dynamics, flow delays, climate-sensitive water availability, and renewable variability. I also aim to study coordination among infrastructure operators with different objectives, information, and incentives, extending beyond centralized planning assumptions.

Scalable optimization and learning-assisted decisions

I plan to develop decomposition, convexification, and reformulation methods for larger infrastructure models. I am also interested in exploring reinforcement learning as a complement to optimization for sequential operating decisions, with attention to physical feasibility and reliability.

Flexibility beyond water and power

I plan to extend these frameworks to gas networks, electric vehicles, and data centers. The aim is to identify flexibility and demand-response opportunities that are missed when infrastructures are modeled independently, and to study their implications for renewable-energy integration, resilience, and long-term energy sufficiency.
